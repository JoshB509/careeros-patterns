Executive questions clean cut analysis

Empirical reference study and repeatable process specification | Josh | 10 November 2026

The final edit of What to Say to Tough Executive Questions shows that the main task is choosing complete takes, then placing safe joins. Silence trimming alone cannot reproduce it. The proposed process should preserve a source time map, synchronize the good microphone, transcribe and align speech, remove abandoned takes, treat nonverbal events separately, and review uncertain joins before rendering.

Reference and measurement method

Reference selection matters. The current project and export map to 15 retained clips and 408.733 s of timeline audio. Older notes describing 14 clips or roughly 413 s are not the current reference. The analysis uses the direct A1 source discontinuities in the final Premiere project, rather than inferring edit decisions from overall duration.

The source is the enhanced Wireless PRO WAV used in the final A1 edit, not a separately verified raw microphone recording. Source PCM was read at 48 kHz. Its 10 ms RMS p10 is -87.61 dBFS and p95 is -21.00 dBFS; these describe an enhanced signal, not the physical room noise floor. Calibrate again if the raw recording or enhancement chain changes.

Premiere stores 254016000000 ticks per second and approximately 8467191533 ticks per nominal 30 fps video frame. Preserve tick and sample coordinates; do not repeatedly round through displayed seconds. The sub-millisecond timing approximations do not justify adding or deleting content.

- For every retained clip, three independent positions were correlated against the export, for 45 total windows. Correlation windows were up to 1.4 s and evaluated at 16 kHz effective sampling. The matching export lag was 21.302 to 21.373 ms. This validates correspondence throughout the sequence but is not an exhaustive sample-by-sample or perceptual review.

- The 28.925 ms difference between timeline end and decoded export duration is recorded explicitly. It is not used to invent a missing edit or to rescale all source timestamps. Container, codec and final-sample behavior require separate checking when implementing export verification.

- Acoustic offsets use nonoverlapping 10 ms RMS windows in the native microphone PCM. At each source cut, the method searches up to two seconds backward or forward for a window above a stated level. Outgoing offset is the time from the last active window end to source out. Incoming offset is the time from source in to the next active window start.

- Offsets therefore measure threshold-level acoustic activity, not speech onset, consonant completion or word alignment accuracy. A 10 ms measurement grid, enhancement artifacts, breaths and quiet consonants affect them. Zero means activity reached the boundary at that threshold, not proof of an audible fault.

- Premiere words were associated with intervals by word midpoint. The mapped final transcript is the retained subset of source words, not an independently transcribed export. Independent source and export ASR should be retained separately and reconciled, not silently substituted for ground truth.

Synchronization and evidence quality

Camera and microphone synchronization

The project places microphone source time 3.6999963 s ahead of camera source time. Six 10 s speech-envelope checks using a 5 ms grid and 25 ms smoothing find 3.705 to 3.710 s, with correlations 0.830 to 0.886. This corroborates the project offset within one nominal 30 fps frame. Different processing creates about 5 to 10 ms acoustic ambiguity; these checks do not prove lip sync, establish drift or justify retiming.

This approximately 3.7 s camera/microphone relationship is separate from the approximately 21.3 ms microphone/export decoded-audio lag. The two offsets must never be substituted for each other.

Three different questions must be kept separate. An amplitude detector can answer only the first one directly. The requested clean cut needs all three, with positive protection for quiet consonants and natural pauses.

Current cutter audit and replay

The current path is helper/dajoshi_cut.py plan_cut into cleancut.acoustic_keeps. Its acoustic stage is an energy-based interval rule, not semantic take selection and not an event classifier.

- It computes 10 ms RMS, smooths power over 50 ms, estimates a p10 noise level and sets auto threshold to p10 + 5 dB, clamped between -65 and -20 dBFS. On this enhanced source the effective automatic threshold is -65 dBFS. It uses p95 as a reference speech level.

- Above-threshold runs separated by less than 150 ms are merged before filtering. All events shorter than 80 ms are dropped even when the optional sounds switch is off. With the switch on, events shorter than 1.2 s and quieter than p95 minus 11 dB are also dropped. These features do not identify coughs.

- Later pause merging and padding can reinclude an event that was previously dropped. Event protection and deletion therefore need an explicit interval representation that survives later stages.

- Defaults are maximum pause 450 ms, lead 80 ms and tail 100 ms. The UI sets lead to 0.8 times tail. The older 150 ms min_cut parameter is not the active algorithm.

- The flubs path uses transcript-word deletion ranges and generic language-model instructions to keep the final complete take while preserving emphasis. It has not learned this editor style, and does not automatically run acoustic cleanup afterward. Its boundary snap searches plus or minus 80 ms for low RMS with 20 ms word guards; its threshold parameter is unused.

Repeatable pipeline specification

The output of this work is a design and testable process, not a deployed edit. The order below preserves enough evidence to explain and reverse every proposed deletion.

1 Ingest and preserve coordinates

Register original camera and microphone files with hashes, channel count, sample rate, rational frame rate, frame count, duration and time-base metadata. Preserve the originals. Define source camera time, microphone PCM sample time and edited output time as distinct coordinates; do not use rounded displayed timecode as the interchange format.

2 Run the green screen pre step without retiming

Background replacement should preserve source timestamps, rational frame rate, frame count and audio. The pre-step emits the processed video plus a sidecar describing its source and timing. If any frames are removed, repeated or retimed, it must emit an explicit piecewise map back to source. Reject a silently retimed result. Image-only processing is not permission to change recording duration.

3 Choose and synchronize the good microphone

Select the intended good mic explicitly or propose it for review. Match multiple separated speech anchors to camera audio, estimate offset and test affine drift. Model camera time as a + b times microphone time. Validate residuals near the beginning, middle and end; use a piecewise map or manual review if residuals exceed a stated tolerance. This recording's 21.3 ms source-to-export lag is not the camera-to-microphone offset and must not be reused as one.

4 Transcribe and align the source

Transcribe the full good-mic source, with word times and uncertainty. Align text back to PCM and retain raw ASR separately from corrected text. Keep short uncertain tokens and nonverbal spans as reviewable evidence. Use camera audio only as a sync or fallback source, rather than replacing a verified mic with mixed export audio.

5 Propose repeated take removal

Group nearby restarts using lexical overlap, syntax, repeated meaning and acoustic pauses. Propose retaining the last complete usable take, but check that earlier material adds no unique fact, qualification or intentional emphasis. The rule is a candidate-generation default, not always keep the last take. Record exactly what is removed and why. Send partial words, mixed complete takes and meaning changes to review.

6 Build a separate event and pause track

Before phrase merging, mark candidate breaths, coughs, throat clears, clicks, silence and uncertain speech on the source time axis. Keep event classification confidence separate from speech alignment confidence. Remove a nonverbal event only when its class and surrounding speech safety meet the approved pilot criteria. Otherwise retain and queue it.

7 Refine joins then export proposals

After semantic deletions, refine the outgoing and incoming sides independently using speech and event evidence. Preserve natural intra-phrase pauses and emphasis. Produce conservative EDL and JSON with source mappings, reason codes, confidence, uncertainty and review snippets. Apply only reviewed proposals to copies, then render and validate the result.

Choosing a clean join

Speech detection and boundary search serve different purposes. A speech-detection threshold helps propose protected speech intervals; a boundary-search level helps evaluate candidate edges only inside a region already judged safe. Do not use one global amplitude number to perform both jobs.

Estimate local noise from validated non-speech windows rather than equating global p10 with the room floor. Use separate onset and offset thresholds, or hysteresis, plus agreement between ASR, voice activity detection, spectral cues and energy to protect speech. These are proposed engineering rules, not evaluated improvements in this study.

Boundary search

- Use alignment to identify the first and last intended phonemes, then extend into safe background. A low-energy valley inside a fricative or quiet syllable is not a safe boundary just because RMS is low.

- Set a bounded search around each proposed word boundary and return multiple scored candidates. Treat short cut leads such as cut 3, and the ambiguous incoming regions at cuts 5 and 13, as required review cases. A 20 ms transcript guard does not establish phonetic safety.

- Separate join placement from pause policy. Between continuous intended phrases, retain the source pause unless a contextual rule proposes shortening it. A numerical ceiling alone cannot decide whether a pause is rhetorical, cognitive or an interruption.

- Keep any confirmed event removal as an explicit excluded interval. Later padding and phrase merging must not reinclude it silently. If safe padding would cross an excluded event or word, flag the conflict rather than overriding one stage invisibly.

- Quantize only at the output stage. Preserve sample-accurate audio ranges internally, derive video boundaries using the verified time map, and report any frame-rounding displacement. Audio and video edit lists must refer to the same intended content.

Refine only within an approved safe non-speech band. Within that band, prefer a low-energy sample crossing and small amplitude discontinuity, while preserving sufficient handles. If needed, test an optional 2 to 5 ms fade confined to non-speech. That duration is a proposed engineering safeguard, not a measured editor preference. Never crossfade syllables or erase breaths implicitly.

The largest measured raw source hard-splice sample jump across the 14 reference joins is -49.25 dBFS, at cut 13. This makes seam discontinuities measurable, but does not establish that a click is or is not audible. A zero-jump case is recorded as -200 dBFS in the diagnostic file; this is a numerical sentinel, not a physical noise reading. Listening and rendered-output checks remain necessary.

Cough and throat clear handling

The current short-and-quiet heuristic is insufficient: coughs can be loud or adjacent to speech, while short quiet events can be legitimate words, consonants or breaths. Merging before classification can absorb an event into speech; later expansion can undo a removal. A separate event track and post-merge exclusion check are required.

A candidate detector may combine event-model scores, transient shape, spectral features, speech probability and ASR alignment. These are proposed inputs, not a trained or evaluated classifier in this study. Keep a configurable policy for natural breaths rather than treating every non-speech event as unwanted.

Build a human-labeled pilot with coughs, throat clears, breaths, clicks, quiet words and clean pauses, including examples adjacent to speech. Tune for preserving intended speech. Select a threshold only after estimating event precision, recall and false deletion rates on held-out recordings. A single reference edit cannot establish reliability.

Candidate events found in this source

Thirteen detectable acoustic events were at least 70% outside Premiere lexical timestamps. Eight fall in removed material and five in retained material. This is deliberately weak candidate selection, not thirteen coughs. Recognition and timing errors or quiet speech can explain some retained candidates.

Priority event 04 has an above-threshold core at source 257.70 to 257.99 s and peak 10 ms RMS -27.14 dBFS. Event 11 has a core at 485.59 to 485.72 s and peak -36.17 dBFS. Both were removed in the reference and are not silence by energy. Neither is a validated cough or throat clear. Event 11 has weak energy extending toward 486.05 s, beyond its core and into the kept side before fuller speech around 486.10 s. Never cut only the detected core and assume the complete event is gone.

Use the thirteen review WAVs and candidate spectrogram atlas to label these sounds by listening. Event labels remain provisional until then.

Review queue fields

For each candidate include source range, camera range, proposed output join, reason, raw transcript context, alignment confidence, event scores, old and proposed padding, threshold sensitivity, competing boundaries, and a short before-and-after audio snippet. Never replace an uncertain label with silence merely because ASR returned no words.

Implementation pseudocode

The following is a proposed contract, not executable production code. Intervals are half-open and source sample indices are authoritative for audio. Every stage emits evidence and reversible decisions.

manifest = inspect_and_hash(camera, good_mic)
green = greenscreen_copy(camera, preserve_timestamps=True)
require_timing_match(green, camera)  # or explicit time map

anchors = match_speech_anchors(good_mic, camera.audio)
sync = fit_offset_and_drift(anchors)
if not sync_passes(sync): queue_sync_review(); stop_edit_apply()
time_map = compose_mic_camera_green_map(sync, green.map)

raw_asr = transcribe(good_mic)
aligned = align_with_uncertainty(raw_asr, good_mic)
events = detect_events_before_phrase_merge(good_mic, aligned)

retakes = propose_restarts(aligned, nearby_context=True)
for proposal in retakes:
    check_complete_take(proposal)
    check_unique_information_and_intentional_emphasis(proposal)
    proposal.boundaries = refine_both_sides(
        good_mic, aligned, events, candidate_pads=[(.10,.15)])
    route_uncertainty_to_review(proposal)

pauses = propose_contextual_pause_changes(aligned, retakes)
event_edits = propose_only_high_confidence_safe_events(events)
plan = combine_intervals(retakes, pauses, event_edits)
plan = resolve_overlap_and_protected_speech(plan)
assert_no_reintroduced_excluded_events(plan)
assert_no_unreviewed_uncertain_speech_deletion(plan)

edl, audit = export_plan(plan, time_map, retain_raw_times=True)
review = export_original_and_proposed_join_snippets(plan)
# Apply only approved plan to copies; originals remain unchanged.
rendered = render_copy(approved(plan), camera, good_mic, green)
validate_content_timing_joins_and_event_policy(rendered, audit)

Minimum plan record

Each decision needs an ID, source asset hash, microphone sample start/end, rational time base, mapped camera frame or timestamp range, retained take ID, reason code, evidence references, confidence and review status. Store measured boundary features separately from proposed settings. Include the exact algorithm version and parameter set so a later run can reproduce the plan.

Suggested reason codes are RETAKE, ABANDONED_START, PAUSE_SHORTEN, COUGH_CANDIDATE, THROAT_CLEAR_CANDIDATE and MANUAL_REVIEW. Only confirmed labels should drop the CANDIDATE suffix. A rejection, keep decision or manual override should also remain in the audit log.

Quality assurance and acceptance

Structural and synchronization checks

- Verify all 15 reference clip mappings and all 14 reference deletions from the saved intervals before changing algorithms. Assert chronological, nonoverlapping ranges and equality between kept plus removed durations and source duration, allowing only documented rounding.

- Check green-screen frame count and timestamps against the original. Test multiple mic/camera anchors and drift residuals. After rendering, compare known speech anchors across the entire output, not just its beginning.

- Report final sample count, audio duration, video duration and cumulative time-map error independently. Do not hide a mismatch by stretching or reinterpreting all timestamps.

Content and listening checks

- Listen to original context and proposed joins at every candidate cut. Check clipped consonants, plosives, repeated fragments, unnatural breath transitions, clicks and pace. The waveform and ASR work in this study cannot certify these perceptual outcomes.

- Confirm that every removed repeated take is redundant and that the retained version is complete. Preserve meaningful hesitation and intentional repetition. Verify no unique fact, qualifier or instruction disappeared.

- Audit every event deletion separately. Review both detected events and a sample of retained material to catch false negatives. Label coughs and throat clears by listening, not transcript absence.

Evaluation design

Use this video for development and explanation, then evaluate on additional held-out raw recordings with different delivery pace, background noise and enhancement settings. Split by recording, not neighboring snippets, to avoid overfitting to one source. Report event precision and recall, false deletions of intended speech, missed retakes, join ratings, reference interval disagreement and review workload separately.

A pilot acceptance gate should require zero confirmed intended-speech deletions in the reviewed pilot, all flagged cases resolved, no unexplained sync drift, and satisfactory listening checks. Zero errors in a finite sample is not a reliability guarantee. Select quantitative tolerance and confidence requirements with the human labels before production deployment.

What is not established

All 14 internal cuts were measured and their raw boundary waveforms inspected, along with both head and tail waveform plots, RMS and event evidence. No full perceptual listening pass was performed in this runtime. Cough and throat-clear classifications remain unverified. RMS offsets are not phonetic labels: uncertainty includes the 10 ms grid, threshold choice and output frame rounding. ASR boundaries can be uncertain by 100 ms or more. ASR can be wrong. Replay disagreements are not definitive semantic errors. The proposed settings, event model and green-screen integration have not been implemented or validated on held-out recordings. No claim of fully automatic editor-quality output follows from this single example.
